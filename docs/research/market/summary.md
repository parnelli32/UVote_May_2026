# Market Research Summary: The Gap UVote Fills

*One-page synthesis. Full detail in `competitive-landscape.md` and `trust-in-government-stats.md`.*

## The gap

No existing product combines all four pieces of UVote's core mechanic: **(1)** automatic address-to-representative matching, **(2)** a live feed of bills across multiple legislative bodies at once (city, state, and eventually federal), **(3)** a citizen vote/opinion on each bill informed by a plain-language summary, and **(4)** a standing, public comparison of that citizen sentiment against how the representative actually voted.

Fourteen products and categories were reviewed — bill-tracking tools (BillTrack50, LegiScan, GovTrack), rep-contact tools (Resistbot, 5 Calls), reference tools (Ballotpedia, Represent API), sentiment/polling apps (Countable, iCitizen, VoteSpotter), municipal engagement software (CivicPlus), official e-voting (Voatz), and Philadelphia's own civic infrastructure (Legistar, Philly311). Every one is missing at least two of the four pieces:

- **LegiScan's free "OneVote"** is the closest analog on the voting mechanic — it genuinely lets a citizen cast support/oppose on a bill — but requires manually selecting your state and officials (no address matching), and produces no aggregated district-level score or public divergence signal.
- **VoteSpotter** is the closest analog on address matching and plain-English alerts, but it's state-legislature-only, and its "feedback" is a one-off reaction sent after the vote already happened, not a persistent, aggregated, publicly comparable record.
- **Resistbot** and **5 Calls** nail effortless, multi-level address-to-rep matching, but neither has any bill-specific vote-casting or a persistent voting record at all — they're built for a single message, not an ongoing relationship with a district's sentiment.
- Nothing in this landscape produces the artifact that makes UVote's model an accountability mechanic rather than just an engagement feature: a **standing, public score comparing a representative's actual vote to their district's aggregated citizen sentiment**, repeated independently at every level of government representing that citizen.

**Why the combination matters more than any single piece:** each capability is nearly worthless without the others. Knowing who represents you (Ballotpedia, Represent, 5 Calls) with nothing to act on is a dead end. Casting an opinion with no persistent record or aggregation (Countable's legacy model, CivicPlus's per-city polls) evaporates the moment the session ends. A one-off reaction after the vote already happened (VoteSpotter) can't function as advocacy with a timestamped, "impossible to claim they never heard" record. Only fusing all four into one simple, low-friction flow produces something genuinely new: a continuous, comparable, cross-jurisdiction record of whether representation is actually working — which is UVote's stated founding premise.

## The 7 strongest supporting stats

1. **Federal trust is at a generational low.** *"Just 17% of Americans now say they trust the government in Washington to do what is right 'just about always' (2%) or 'most of the time' (15%)."* — Pew Research Center, Dec 4, 2025.
2. **But local government is still trusted — this is where UVote should build first.** *"Americans have the most faith in local government (67%) and the least faith in the legislative branch of the federal government, or Congress (32%)."* — Gallup, Oct 13, 2023. This is the strongest data point for the pitch: the erosion of trust is concentrated at the federal level, exactly where UVote isn't starting. Philadelphia City Council sits on the most trusted layer of government in the country.
3. **The "not being heard" problem is not a vague feeling — it's near-universal and measured.** *"More than eight-in-ten U.S. adults [say] most elected officials don't care what people like them think."* — Pew Research Center, Apr 30, 2024.
4. **Congress specifically fails the listening test by its own constituents' account.** *"At least three-quarters or more say that members of Congress do a somewhat or very bad job at listening to the concerns of people in their district (76%)."* — Pew Research Center, Sep 19, 2023. This is the exact gap the Constituent score is built to make checkable rather than anecdotal.
5. **Confidence government can even solve problems is collapsing further than trust itself.** *"Only 11% expressing confidence [in government's ability to address the public's priorities], down slightly from 15% last year."* — AP-NORC, Dec 19, 2025.
6. **The civic information gap is real and asymmetric by age** — exactly the accessibility risk UVote's design principles must guard against. *"Fewer than half of U.S. adults (45%) say it is very or somewhat easy to find the information they need to make voting decisions in local elections. By comparison, 59% say it is easy to find the information they need for presidential elections."* — Pew Research Center (Pew-Knight Initiative), Jul 24, 2024.
7. **Municipal turnout is a fraction of federal/state turnout, meaning most citizens have zero standing signal at the level of government UVote starts with.** *"The other contests clustered around 20 to 35 percent turnout of registered voters – fairly typical of most big-city mayor contests. But even these paltry numbers are deceptively high, since many eligible citizens don't even register to vote."* — Knight Foundation, reporting Portland State University's "Who Votes for Mayor?" study, Jul 28, 2015.

## Why this matters for UVote's pitch

Together, these two findings tell a coherent story a pitch deck can build on directly: **(a)** citizens have essentially given up on federal government listening to them, but still meaningfully trust the local layer — the exact layer where UVote is launching first, and **(b)** that local layer is simultaneously the one with the least standardized information, the lowest turnout, and (per the competitive landscape) zero existing products that let a citizen turn up-close civic participation into a lasting, comparable record. UVote isn't proposing to fix federal-level cynicism; it's proposing to build the missing feedback loop at precisely the level where trust is highest, turnout is lowest, and no competitor has built the address-matched, multi-body, vote-and-compare mechanic that would close that gap.

## What Countable and POPVOX teach UVote

Two deep-dive case studies (`countable-case-study.md`, `popvox-case-study.md`) trace the full histories of the two closest historical analogs to UVote's consumer product — both were founded as free, citizen-facing, bill-specific advocacy tools, and both are, today, no longer that.

**Countable's biggest risk for UVote: consumer-only civic engagement has not proven durable on its own, twice over.** Countable ran its free citizen-voting product from 2013/14 to 2020 before pivoting entirely to paid B2B "brand community" tooling (Starbucks, Uber, Patagonia), and was ultimately acquired in 2024 by an employee-engagement company with no civic mission at all. Its own CEO's explanation was structural, not a failure of mission: free viral distribution to citizens dried up, and the company only found a payer once it stopped charging citizens (who were never paying) and started charging institutions. The civic-tech IP it absorbed along the way, Brigade (Sean Parker-backed, $9.3M Series A), failed for the same underlying reason — as TechCrunch put it, "consumer-focused apps for improving democracy are tough to monetize." **The risk for UVote is not hypothetical: it is the same shape of business UVote is proposing, and it has already failed once in this exact form.** UVote's answer — a B2B official/office tier built into the plan from day one, not discovered after the citizen product stalled — is a direct, deliberate response to this documented failure mode, not a hedge against a risk that might exist.

**POPVOX's biggest risk for UVote: even a moderately successful citizen product can be quietly abandoned once a more fundable adjacent opportunity appears — no dramatic failure required.** Unlike Countable, POPVOX's original consumer advocacy tool (400,000+ users by 2014, Library of Congress archiving its output, a House-Democrats intranet integration) was never publicly shut down in one visible event. It appears to have simply been deprioritized: POPVOX Foundation spun off in 2021 as a separate legislature-facing nonprofit, and POPVOX Inc. itself now markets exclusively as an "institutional AI" infrastructure company selling to governments and legislative staff — the original citizen "read a bill, register your position, it's emailed to your rep" flow is gone from the current product lineup entirely, and its legacy URL now 404s. **This is the more dangerous pattern of the two for UVote to guard against**, because it doesn't require the product to fail — it only requires a better-funded adjacent opportunity to appear once the team has real government relationships and real constituent data (both of which UVote's own roadmap explicitly plans to build). UVote's founding documents treat the citizen product as the permanent core of the mission, not a stepping-stone — but that is a stated intent today, not yet a structural guarantee, and POPVOX's history is a direct, documented example of how that kind of intent can erode over a long enough timeline.
