# Trust in Government: Supporting Data

All statistics below were pulled directly from the cited primary source (fetched and verified against the live page/PDF, not paraphrased secondhand) on 2026-08-04. Each entry gives the source, exact publication date, and a direct quote or exact figure — never a paraphrased number.

---

## 1. Trust in the federal government

### Federal trust is near a 67-year low
> "Just 17% of Americans now say they trust the government in Washington to do what is right 'just about always' (2%) or 'most of the time' (15%)."

- **Source:** Pew Research Center, *"Public Trust in Government: 1958-2025"*
- **Published:** December 4, 2025
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/12/04/public-trust-in-government-1958-2025/
- **Additional context (same report):** this is down from 22% one year earlier, and the same fact sheet describes the current reading as "one of the lowest in the nearly seven decades since the question was first asked by the National Election Study."

### The partisan split is historically unusual right now
> "Democrats' trust in the government has never been lower: Just 9% now say they trust the federal government just about always or most of the time."

> "Today 26% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents say they trust the federal government – on par with GOP views during the first Trump administration."

- **Source:** Pew Research Center, *"Public Trust in Government: 1958-2025"*
- **Published:** December 4, 2025
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/12/04/public-trust-in-government-1958-2025/

### Confidence that government can actually address people's priorities is even lower
> "The public is highly pessimistic about the ability of government to address their priorities, with only 11% expressing confidence, down slightly from 15% last year. Republicans are more confident than independents or Democrats (25% vs 5% vs 2%)."

- **Source:** AP-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research, *"2026: The public's priorities and expectations"*
- **Published:** December 19, 2025 (survey fieldwork December 4–8, 2025)
- **URL:** https://apnorc.org/projects/2026-the-publics-priorities-and-expectations/

## 2. Trust in local vs. state vs. federal government

### Local government is the most trusted level; Congress the least
> "Americans have the most faith in local government (67%) and the least faith in the legislative branch of the federal government, or Congress (32%). Between these two extremes, majorities express trust in state government and the American people."

> "Trust in each institution or actor is statistically similar to a year ago, except for Congress, which saw a drop of six percentage points, from 38% expressing a great deal or fair amount of trust in it last year to 32% this year."

- **Source:** Gallup, *"Americans Trust Local Government Most, Congress Least"* (Jeffrey M. Jones), based on Gallup's annual Governance survey (fielded Sept. 1–23, 2023)
- **Published:** October 13, 2023
- **URL:** https://news.gallup.com/poll/512651/americans-trust-local-government-congress-least.aspx

**Why this matters for UVote:** this is the single clearest evidence that the trust gap isn't "citizens hate all government" — it's specifically federal/legislative trust that's collapsed, while the local layer (the layer UVote's Philadelphia pilot starts with) still commands majority trust. That's the layer where a new accountability mechanic has the most receptive audience.

## 3. Feeling heard / represented

### More than 8 in 10 Americans believe elected officials don't care what they think
> "More than eight-in-ten U.S. adults said this in a spring 2023 Pew Research Center survey, compared with a median of 74% of adults across the 24 countries surveyed."

- **Source:** Pew Research Center, *"More than 80% of Americans believe elected officials don't care what people like them think"* (Jenn Hatfield)
- **Published:** April 30, 2024
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/04/30/more-than-80-of-americans-believe-elected-officials-dont-care-what-people-like-them-think/

### Three-quarters say Congress does a bad job listening to their district
> "At least three-quarters or more say that members of Congress do a somewhat or very bad job at listening to the concerns of people in their district (76%), keeping their personal financial interests separate from their work in Congress (81%), working with members of the opposing party (84%) and taking responsibility for their actions (86%)."

- **Source:** Pew Research Center, *"What people think are the biggest problems with Congress and other elected officials"* (part of the report *"Americans' Dismal Views of the Nation's Politics"*)
- **Published:** September 19, 2023
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2023/09/19/evaluations-of-members-of-congress-and-the-biggest-problem-with-elected-officials-today/

### Belief that Congress cares about constituents has fallen sharply
> "In 2018, half of adults said members of Congress care about the people they represent all, most or some of the time. Today, 38% say the same."

> "Five years ago, 47% said congressional members do this [promote laws and policies that serve the public interest] at least some of the time. Today, just 36% say that members of Congress promote the public interest."

- **Source:** Pew Research Center, *"What people think are the biggest problems with Congress and other elected officials"*
- **Published:** September 19, 2023
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2023/09/19/evaluations-of-members-of-congress-and-the-biggest-problem-with-elected-officials-today/

**Why this matters for UVote:** this is the direct empirical gap the Constituent score is built to close. Citizens don't currently have any standardized, checkable way to see whether "my rep doesn't listen to me" is true for a specific vote — they only have this kind of generalized, diffuse survey sentiment.

## 4. Civic information gap (local elections)

### Nearly 4 in 10 Americans can't easily find the local voting information they need
> "Fewer than half of U.S. adults (45%) say it is very or somewhat easy to find the information they need to make voting decisions in local elections. By comparison, 59% say it is easy to find the information they need for presidential elections."

> "But among Americans who get news on local politics, only a quarter are highly satisfied with the quality of the news they get."

- **Source:** Pew Research Center, *"How Americans Get Local Political News"* (part of the Pew-Knight Initiative, a research program funded jointly by the Pew Charitable Trusts and the John S. and James L. Knight Foundation)
- **Published:** July 24, 2024
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/07/24/how-americans-get-local-political-news/

### Older adults are far more plugged into local political news than younger adults
> "71% of U.S. adults ages 18 to 29 get news about local government and politics from social media often or sometimes, compared with 36% of those 65 and older. Conversely, Americans 65 and older are more inclined than adults under 30 to get local political news from local news outlets (75% vs. 53%) and from local politicians (39% vs. 20%)."

- **Source:** Pew Research Center, *"How Americans Get Local Political News"*
- **Published:** July 24, 2024
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/07/24/how-americans-get-local-political-news/

**Why this matters for UVote:** this is direct evidence for the "no vote without context" design principle — the information gap on local civic matters is real and measured, and it's exactly the gap UVote's plain-language, Grade-8 bill summaries are built to close before a vote is ever cast. It's also a warning: an app that only reaches digitally-engaged younger users risks widening, not narrowing, the exact participation gap the Design Principles call out.

### Local news is more trusted than national news, but the trust gap is narrower than assumed
> "More Americans trust local news than national news. Forty-five percent of Americans trust reporting by local news organizations 'a great deal' or 'quite a lot,' compared with 31% for national news organizations."

- **Source:** Knight Foundation / Gallup, *"State of Public Trust in Local News"*
- **Published:** October 2019
- **URL:** https://knightfoundation.org/reports/state-of-public-trust-in-local-news/ (full PDF: https://kf-site-production.s3.amazonaws.com/media_elements/files/000/000/440/original/State_of_Public_Trust_in_Local_Media_final_.pdf)

## 5. Civic participation (voter turnout)

### Municipal election turnout is dramatically lower than turnout in federal/state races
> "Voter turnout for mayor elections in these four cities – Portland, Ore., and the Knight communities of Charlotte, N.C., Detroit and St. Paul, Minn. – ranged from a low of just 7 percent (Charlotte's primary) to a high of 72 percent (Portland's general election, one of the few in the U.S. held coincident with the November 2012 presidential election). The other contests clustered around 20 to 35 percent turnout of registered voters – fairly typical of most big-city mayor contests. But even these paltry numbers are deceptively high, since many eligible citizens don't even register to vote."

- **Source:** Knight Foundation, reporting on Portland State University's Population Research Center / Center for Public Service pilot study, *"Who Votes for Mayor?"*
- **Published:** July 28, 2015
- **URL:** https://knightfoundation.org/articles/portland-state-study-seniors-much-more-likely-cast-ballots-young-votes/

**Why this matters for UVote:** this is the primary evidentiary basis for the "turnout in local and municipal elections regularly falls below 20%" claim in `CLAUDE.md`, and it independently corroborates the project's founding premise — an election selects a person for these low-turnout, low-information local races, without any structured mechanism for the much larger group of eligible-but-non-voting citizens to weigh in between elections. Note: this is the most recent primary academic study located during this research; a more recent nationwide dataset on municipal turnout specifically was not found and verified in this pass — if a more current figure is needed for the pitch deck, this should be re-checked against Portland State's ongoing "Who Votes for Mayor?" tracking or a current NCSL brief before publication.
