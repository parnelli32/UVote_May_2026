# Legislative Information Access, Trust, and Informed Participation

UVote's "no vote without context" principle rests on an empirical bet: that giving citizens plain-language, honest information about a bill (not the raw bill text) is enough to produce meaningful participation, and that the absence of this information is itself a major driver of low trust and low engagement. This theme covers the evidence for and around that bet.

---

## Low-information voters can act like well-informed ones if given the right shortcuts - full policy literacy is not required

**Citation:** Lupia, A. (1994). "Shortcuts Versus Encyclopedias: Information and Voting Behavior in California Insurance Reform Elections." *American Political Science Review*, 88(1), 63-76. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/shortcuts-versus-encyclopedias-information-and-voting-behavior-in-california-insurance-reform-elections/75776D189E80711664254A02BC0573F4

**Summary:** Studying California's 1988 insurance-reform ballot initiatives, Lupia found that voters who knew only credible, simple cues about who supported/opposed a measure voted the same way as voters who had studied the substantive policy details in depth. Being an "encyclopedia" of policy knowledge was not necessary for voters to behave as if they were well-informed - a well-chosen shortcut was functionally equivalent.

**How this supports UVote:** This is the foundational academic justification for the Grade-8 reading level plain-language bill summary as sufficient informational context for a legitimate vote, rather than a compromise or a "dumbed-down" version of the real thing. It directly supports the product decision to gate voting on reading a short honest summary (what it does, who it affects, what happens if it fails) rather than requiring or displaying the full bill text as the primary artifact.

## Local news decline is causally linked to lower local political knowledge, lower turnout, and less responsive local government

**Citation:** Hayes, D., & Lawless, J. L. (2021). *News Hole: The Demise of Local Journalism and Political Engagement*. Cambridge University Press. https://www.amazon.com/News-Hole-Journalism-Engagement-Communication/dp/1108834779

**Summary:** Analyzing fifteen years of coverage across 200+ local newspapers alongside election returns, surveys, and journalist interviews, Hayes and Lawless show that when local political coverage shrinks, citizens become less able to evaluate their representatives, less likely to hold opinions about local candidates, and less likely to vote - and the officials themselves become less responsive. The mechanism is a straightforward information supply problem: citizens can't engage with what they never see reported.

**How this supports UVote:** This validates the core "missing layer" framing of UVote's business model: local political information genuinely has disappeared from most citizens' daily information diet, and the product isn't competing with an existing well-functioning information channel, it's filling a structural void left by local newsroom collapse. It supports positioning UVote (in the pitch deck) as a substitute source of last-mile local political information, not merely an "engagement app" competing with existing civic tools.

## Trust in government is at a multi-decade low, and most Americans believe government is not transparent

**Citation:** Pew Research Center. (2019). "Key findings about Americans' declining trust in government and each other." https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2019/07/22/key-findings-about-americans-declining-trust-in-government-and-each-other/

**Summary:** Pew's long-running trust tracking shows public trust in the federal government has fallen from roughly 77% in the late 1950s to under 30% consistently since 2007 (22% as of the most recent survey cited). Related Pew/Pew Charitable Trusts reporting finds only about 15% of Americans believe government is transparent, and fewer than half believe it is accountable to the public.

**How this supports UVote:** Establishes the scale of the trust deficit UVote's accountability mechanic (the Constituent Score, and the public prompt for officials to explain divergent votes) is aimed at closing. This is useful pitch-deck framing for the market opportunity: the problem isn't a niche civic-engagement gap, it's a structural, decades-long, near-universal erosion of institutional trust that a transparent, documented record of alignment is well-positioned to partially address.

## Elected officials are frequently and systematically wrong about what their constituents actually want

**Citation:** Broockman, D. E., & Skovron, C. (2018). "Bias in Perceptions of Public Opinion among Political Elites." *American Political Science Review*, 112(3), 542-563. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/bias-in-perceptions-of-public-opinion-among-political-elites/2EF080E04D3AAE6AC1C894F52642E706

**Summary:** Surveying 3,765 state legislative candidates and officials on their perceptions of constituent opinion across nine policy issues, the authors found politicians of both parties, but especially those contacted disproportionately by ideologically unrepresentative constituents, substantially and systematically misjudge what their own district actually believes. The bias is large enough to plausibly distort real legislative behavior.

**How this supports UVote:** This is direct evidence that the information gap UVote claims to fill is real and consequential, not assumed: representatives do not have an accurate, low-cost, representative channel for constituent opinion today, and instead rely on whoever contacts them (a systematically biased sample). This supports both the B2B/official-tier pitch (the alignment dashboard is solving a real information deficiency for the official, not just a transparency tool aimed against them) and the emphasis on statistical rigor (minimum vote thresholds, resistance to manipulation) in how UVote's aggregated scores are computed, since a biased or gameable signal would simply reproduce the same problem it's meant to fix.
