# Relevant Academics and Professors

Two lists of real, independently verified researchers whose published work bears directly on UVote's premise: national scholars whose findings underpin claims made elsewhere in this folder, and Philadelphia-area scholars (prioritizing UPenn) who are geographically positioned to evaluate or partner on UVote's own launch market. Every person below was confirmed via their institutional faculty page, personal academic site, or a matching university news profile - not from memory. Where a person's current role required a caveat (e.g., no longer primarily an academic), that is noted rather than glossed over.

Both lists reached the full 10 with solidly verified people.

---

## List 1: National Academics

### David E. Broockman - University of California, Berkeley
Associate Professor of Political Science and Director of the Center for American Democracy, Travers Department of Political Science.
**Why relevant:** Co-author of the elite-misperception study that grounds UVote's framing of the Constituent Score as filling a real information gap for officials (see `representation-and-accountability.md`), not manufacturing conflict where none exists.
**Representative publication:** Broockman, D. E., & Skovron, C. (2018). "Bias in Perceptions of Public Opinion among Political Elites." *American Political Science Review*, 112(3), 542-563.
**Website:** https://vcresearch.berkeley.edu/faculty/david-broockman

### Christopher Skovron
PhD, University of Michigan (2017). Currently a research scientist in industry (Meta) and an external faculty affiliate of the Northwestern Institute on Complex Systems - flagged here as no longer a primary academic appointment, though the underlying research remains peer-reviewed and citable.
**Why relevant:** Co-author of the same elite-misperception study above; his dissertation and subsequent research specifically measured how state legislators misjudge their own district's opinion, the exact gap UVote's Constituent Score is designed to close.
**Representative publication:** Broockman, D. E., & Skovron, C. (2018). "Bias in Perceptions of Public Opinion among Political Elites." *American Political Science Review*, 112(3), 542-563.
**Website:** https://chrisskovron.com

### Donald P. Green - Columbia University
J.W. Burgess Professor of Political Science.
**Why relevant:** Co-author of the field experiment showing social/comparative visibility of turnout is one of the largest known levers on voter participation - direct support for prioritizing the District Score's visibility on the voting screen itself (see `civic-engagement-and-turnout.md`).
**Representative publication:** Gerber, A. S., Green, D. P., & Larimer, C. W. (2008). "Social Pressure and Voter Turnout: Evidence from a Large-Scale Field Experiment." *American Political Science Review*, 102(1), 33-48. Also: Green, D. P., & Gerber, A. S. *Get Out the Vote: How to Increase Voter Turnout* (Brookings Institution Press).
**Website:** https://polisci.columbia.edu/content/donald-p-green

### Alan S. Gerber - Yale University
Sterling Professor of Political Science; Director, Institution for Social and Policy Studies.
**Why relevant:** Co-author of both the social-pressure turnout experiment and the descriptive-norms messaging study, together the strongest evidentiary basis for UVote's UX-copy rule of framing participation stats positively ("X have voted") rather than around shortfalls.
**Representative publication:** Gerber, A. S., & Rogers, T. (2009). "Descriptive Social Norms and Motivation to Vote: Everybody's Voting and So Should You." *The Journal of Politics*, 71(1), 178-191.
**Website:** https://campuspress.yale.edu/alangerber/

### Daniel M. Butler - Washington University in St. Louis
Professor of Political Science.
**Why relevant:** Author of the field experiment demonstrating legislators shift their votes toward constituent opinion when given accurate, district-specific data - the single strongest causal evidence that UVote's core mechanic can plausibly change real legislative behavior, not just create a public record after the fact.
**Representative publication:** Butler, D. M., & Nickerson, D. W. (2011). "Can Learning Constituency Opinion Affect How Legislators Vote? Results from a Field Experiment." *Quarterly Journal of Political Science*, 6(1), 55-83. Also: Butler, D. M. *Representing the Advantaged: How Politicians Reinforce Inequality* (Cambridge University Press).
**Website:** https://polisci.washu.edu/people/daniel-butler

### Sarah F. Anzia - University of California, Berkeley
Professor of Public Policy and Political Science, Goldman School of Public Policy.
**Why relevant:** Her research on off-cycle local elections structurally suppressing and skewing turnout supports UVote's argument for measuring success via continuous, year-round engagement rather than Election Day turnout, and for framing UVote as extending to ordinary citizens the same persistent-influence channel organized groups already have (see `local-vs-national-engagement.md`).
**Representative publication:** Anzia, S. F. (2013). *Timing and Turnout: How Off-Cycle Elections Favor Organized Groups*. University of Chicago Press. Also: Anzia, S. F. (2022). *Local Interests: Politics, Policy, and Interest Groups in US City Governments*. University of Chicago Press.
**Website:** https://gspp.berkeley.edu/research-and-impact/faculty/sarah-anzia

### Zoltan L. Hajnal - University of California, San Diego
Professor of Political Science, School of Global Policy and Strategy.
**Why relevant:** His research shows election timing alone explains roughly half the variance in municipal turnout, and that low local turnout produces racially and economically skewed representation in city outcomes - directly supports treating Philadelphia's off-cycle municipal elections as a structural (not attitudinal) participation problem UVote is positioned to address between elections.
**Representative publication:** Hajnal, Z. L., & Lewis, P. G. (2003). "Municipal Institutions and Voter Turnout in Local Elections." *Urban Affairs Review*, 38(5), 645-668. Also: Hajnal, Z. L. (2020). *America's Uneven Democracy: Race, Turnout, and Representation in City Politics*. Cambridge University Press.
**Website:** https://gps.ucsd.edu/faculty-directory/zoltan-hajnal.html

### Paul G. Lewis - Arizona State University
Associate Professor, School of Politics and Global Studies.
**Why relevant:** Co-author of the seminal study finding that on-cycle local elections have turnout 25-36 points higher than off-cycle ones - foundational evidence for why formal Election Day turnout is the wrong yardstick for civic interest in Philadelphia city government specifically.
**Representative publication:** Hajnal, Z. L., & Lewis, P. G. (2003). "Municipal Institutions and Voter Turnout in Local Elections." *Urban Affairs Review*, 38(5), 645-668.
**Website:** https://search.asu.edu/profile/842643

### Todd Rogers - Harvard Kennedy School
Weatherhead Professor of Public Policy; Faculty Director, Behavioral Insights Group.
**Why relevant:** Co-author of both the "voting plan" implementation-intentions experiment and the descriptive-norms messaging study - together the direct evidence base for prioritizing low-friction voting flows (one-tap voting, address autofill) and positively framed participation copy over generic reminders or content-heavy engagement features.
**Representative publication:** Nickerson, D. W., & Rogers, T. (2010). "Do You Have a Voting Plan? Implementation Intentions, Voter Turnout, and Organic Plan Making." *Psychological Science*, 21(2), 194-199.
**Website:** https://www.toddthings.com

### Danny Hayes - George Washington University
Professor of Political Science and (by courtesy) Media and Public Affairs.
**Why relevant:** Co-author (with Jennifer L. Lawless, University of Virginia) of the definitive study linking local newsroom collapse to declining local political knowledge and turnout - the strongest available support for UVote's "missing layer" business-model framing: the product is replacing a vanished information channel, not competing with a healthy one.
**Representative publication:** Hayes, D., & Lawless, J. L. (2021). *News Hole: The Demise of Local Journalism and Political Engagement*. Cambridge University Press.
**Website:** https://www.dannyhayes.org

## List 2: Philadelphia-Area Academics (UPenn-Prioritized)

### Marc Meredith - University of Pennsylvania
Professor of Political Science, with a secondary appointment in Business Economics and Public Policy at the Wharton School; affiliated with the Penn Program on Opinion Research and Election Studies (PORES).
**Why relevant:** His research on election administration and convenience voting (how procedural friction affects who actually casts a ballot) is directly applicable to UVote's own voting-flow design decisions, and he is a Philadelphia-based election-behavior researcher well positioned for a future partnered evaluation of UVote's own turnout claims.
**Representative publication:** Meredith, M., & Malhotra, N. "Convenience Voting Can Affect Election Outcomes." *Election Law Journal*, 10(3), 227-253.
**Website:** https://pores.upenn.edu/people/marc-meredith

### Kathleen Hall Jamieson - University of Pennsylvania
Director, Annenberg Public Policy Center; Elizabeth Ware Packard Professor, Annenberg School for Communication.
**Why relevant:** Her Center's annual Constitution Day civics knowledge survey is the leading standing measure of Americans' baseline civic literacy - directly relevant to validating (or benchmarking against) the assumption behind UVote's "no vote without context" principle, that most citizens lack basic civic/legislative knowledge absent a plain-language intermediary.
**Representative publication:** Annenberg Public Policy Center. *Annenberg Constitution Day Civics Survey* (annual, ongoing).
**Website:** https://www.annenbergpublicpolicycenter.org/

### Diana C. Mutz - University of Pennsylvania
Samuel A. Stouffer Professor of Political Science and Communication; Director, Institute for the Study of Citizens and Politics.
**Why relevant:** Her work on how ordinary citizens form political opinions and relate to the political world bears on how UVote should present bill information and framing to avoid distorting the "honest, non-partisan" bill summary the product's design principles require.
**Representative publication:** Mutz, D. C. *Hearing the Other Side: Deliberative versus Participatory Democracy*. Cambridge University Press.
**Website:** https://www.asc.upenn.edu/people/faculty/diana-c-mutz-phd

### John Lapinski - University of Pennsylvania
Robert A. Fox Leadership Professor of Political Science; Faculty Director, Penn Program on Opinion Research and Election Studies (PORES); Director of the Elections Unit, NBC News.
**Why relevant:** As Penn's lead election-data researcher (and NBC's election-night analytics director), he represents the most plausible academic partner for validating UVote's district-level vote aggregation methodology and for a future rigorous-evaluation partnership, per the "no completed outcome study of a comparable tool exists yet" gap noted in `civic-tech-ux-patterns.md`. He is also the co-author of the direct, citable critique of DW-NOMINATE flagged in `dw-nominate.md` - see `lapinski-deep-dive.md` for a full reading of that paper and a prioritized, concrete engagement plan (not a generic "plausible partner" note).
**Representative publication:** Lapinski, J. S. *The Substance of Representation: Congress, American Political Development, and Lawmaking* (Princeton University Press).
**Website:** https://pores.upenn.edu/people/john-lapinski/

### Matthew Levendusky - University of Pennsylvania
Professor of Political Science and the Annenberg School for Communication; Stephen and Mary Baran Chair in the Institutions of Democracy, Annenberg Public Policy Center; former Faculty Director, Fels Institute of Government (2018-2023).
**Why relevant:** His research on what reduces partisan animosity, and his direct experience running Penn's own local-government training institute, together support UVote's "non-partisan by architecture" design principle and are relevant to how the product should (and should not) present party or ideological cues alongside bill and vote information.
**Representative publication:** Levendusky, M. S. (2023). *Our Common Bonds: Using What Americans Share to Overcome the Partisan Divide*. University of Chicago Press.
**Website:** https://web.sas.upenn.edu/mleven/

### Yphtach Lelkes - University of Pennsylvania
Professor, Annenberg School for Communication (secondary appointment in Political Science); Co-Director, Polarization Research Lab.
**Why relevant:** Studies how the political information environment (news, social media, communication design) shapes attitudes and polarization - directly relevant to auditing whether UVote's own UI (bill summaries, sponsor information, vote displays) could inadvertently prime partisan reactions rather than neutral policy evaluation.
**Representative publication:** Lelkes, Y., et al. Research on affective polarization and the political information environment (see full list at website below).
**Website:** https://www.ylelkes.com

### David W. Nickerson - Temple University
Professor of Political Science.
**Why relevant:** A Philadelphia-based pioneer of field-experimental voter mobilization research (over a thousand field experiments conducted), and co-author of the "voting plan" friction-reduction study cited in `civic-engagement-and-turnout.md` - directly relevant both as a methodological resource and as a potential local evaluation partner for UVote's own engagement mechanics.
**Representative publication:** Nickerson, D. W., & Rogers, T. (2010). "Do You Have a Voting Plan? Implementation Intentions, Voter Turnout, and Organic Plan Making." *Psychological Science*, 21(2), 194-199.
**Website:** https://sites.temple.edu/nickerson/

### Michael W. Sances - Temple University
Associate Professor of Political Science.
**Why relevant:** His research specifically studies the limits of local government responsiveness to voters and how citizens attribute credit/blame across levels of government - directly relevant to UVote's multi-jurisdiction design (separate alignment scores per legislative body) and to setting realistic expectations for how much a transparency tool can move official behavior at the local level.
**Representative publication:** Sances, M. W. (2021). "When Voters Matter: The Limits of Local Government Responsiveness." *Urban Affairs Review*.
**Website:** https://sites.temple.edu/msances/

### Richardson Dilworth - Drexel University
Professor and Interim Department Head, Department of Politics; Director, Drexel Center for Public Policy.
**Why relevant:** The most directly Philadelphia-focused scholar on this list - his research and edited volumes on Philadelphia's own civic and political history are the most relevant available academic grounding for any claim UVote makes about Philadelphia's specific civic landscape being structurally underserved.
**Representative publication:** Dilworth, R. (Ed.) (2006). *Social Capital in the City: Community and Civic Life in Philadelphia*. Temple University Press.
**Website:** https://drexel.edu/coas/faculty-research/faculty-directory/politics/Dilworth-Richardson/

### Robert P. Inman - University of Pennsylvania (Wharton School)
Richard King Mellon Professor Emeritus of Finance, Economics, and Public Policy. Emeritus status noted; he continues to teach at Wharton and Penn Law.
**Why relevant:** A leading scholar of fiscal federalism and local public finance - relevant to the substance of many bills UVote users will actually be voting on (city budgets, tax and spending measures), and to the pitch-deck argument that local government decisions carry direct, material stakes for constituents even when turnout is low.
**Representative publication:** Inman, R. P., & Rubinfeld, D. L. (2020). *Democratic Federalism: The Economics, Politics, and Law of Federal Governance*. Princeton University Press.
**Website:** https://bepp.wharton.upenn.edu/profile/inman/
